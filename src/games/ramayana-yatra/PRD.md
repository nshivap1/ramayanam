## Learning objective
After playing through the six kāṇḍas, a learner can read, recognise, and recite 21 famous ślokas of Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa in Devanagari. They know the meaning of every word in them (≈200 lemmas). They can undo common sandhi at word junctions, name the role (vibhakti) of key words, and recognise the anuṣṭubh pattern of 4 × 8 syllables. Their reading ability moves from Praveśa toward Śikṣā (Samskrita Bharati ladder) through a canonical text, not textbook sentences. With the optional Sambhāṣaṇa story text, they also read about 920 lines of simple spoken Sanskrit (Level 1–3 vocabulary and grammar). That's a large dose of comprehensible input bridging everyday Sanskrit and Vālmīki's verses.

## Why people come back
- A story with momentum: six chapters, bosses, a rival, allies who join (Hanumān's friendly duel is the "catch").
- Collection: the Padakośa (words with mastery tiers अदृष्ट → दृष्ट → ज्ञात → सिद्ध) and the Ślokakośa (verses heard → restored in gold).
- Power fantasy tied to learning: restoring a verse unlocks an astra, and swift, correct answers land *śuddha* critical hits.
- Spaced repetition hides inside combat: weak words come back as the challenges of your attacks.
- A persistent save. Every session ends with a result card listing the words practised.

## Core loop
1. **Explore** a pixel-art map (D-pad or arrows; A to talk). The objective line says where to go next.
2. **Hear** a śloka where the story speaks it. The verse card shows four pādas, a word-by-word table, our translation, the context, and the source reference. Its words become *seen*.
3. **Battle**: walking in kuśa grass or meeting a boss starts a turn-based fight. Choose Fight / Switch / Item / Run. Choosing a move asks that move's Sanskrit challenge (artha · lipi · pūraṇa · krama · viccheda · vibhakti) drawn from words and verses you have met.
   - Correct = full power.
   - Correct and swift (≤ 40% of the timer, or first try in Relaxed mode) = ×1.5 *śuddha*.
   - Wrong = ×0.35. The feedback bar then shows the right answer and a one-line explanation.
   - Foes attack back. Win for XP and niṣka. Lose (unless the story allows it) and you wake at the last āśrama.
4. **Rest at an āśrama shrine**: heal, save, and **recite** a heard verse. Recitation is one word-order challenge per pāda plus two word questions; ≥ 75% restores the verse and unlocks its astra.
5. **Pass the chapter gate**: each kāṇḍa's key verse must be restored before its final scene. Beat the boss, and the chapter card for the next kāṇḍa appears. Completing a kāṇḍa shows the in-game **book-complete screen** (v0.5a): the book's name (बालकाण्डं समाप्तम्), one line of what happened, the three numbers explained in the player's language (verses heard, lines recited — or right answers in the practice game — and verses restored, with what each means and why it matters), the book's verses with their state (and the astra a verse gives), and two ways on: **continue to the next book** (named, अयोध्याकाण्डं प्रति; Enter / A) keeps playing in the same session straight into the next book's opening (logic `next-book`: the player is put on the next book's start map and its opening event runs); **stop here** (अद्य अलम्) ends the session with the result screen (outcome *completed*), and the save resumes in the next book. The menu also offers **Rest & end session** at any time. Defeating Rāvaṇa and reaching the epilogue ends with outcome *won*.


### Two journeys (v0.4): Kathā (story first) and Abhyāsa (practice)
The New-journey screen (and Settings) offers two styles; संस्कृतम् and संस्कृतम् + English default to **Kathā**, English to **Abhyāsa**. Abhyāsa is the loop above, unchanged since v0.3. Kathā is for readers who want the epic and its verses rather than word drills (spec and audited interaction rules: `notes/katha.md`):
- **No random encounters and no quizzes.** Story quizzes are skipped (their story effects still happen); party levels have a floor per chapter, so nobody needs to grind.
- **Verses at their moments.** Each verse appears where the story speaks it as पदच्छेदः → अन्वयः (the words unfold into prose order) → सरलार्थः (a plain-Sanskrit meaning), with English in संस्कृतम् + English.
- **Astras are cast by reciting.** A verse-bound astra recites one pāda of its verse: the pāda is printed in chunks and at most one word is missing; one tap fills it (a miss still fires at half power, nothing is timed). Each astra rotates through every pāda of its verse, so a playthrough recites the whole text; a mastered pāda recites itself, with spot checks on casts 2, 4, 8, 16 …
- **One-tap battles (v0.4.1).** The command panel lists the hero's moves; one tap casts. A newly unlocked astra is equipped automatically, the hero holding the most astras leads each story fight, and foe HP is scaled so a story fight takes ≈ 6–10 turns and a boss ≈ 7–14. Duels go straight to the recitation. No story fight can be lost by a reasonable player (bots: 0 losses over 75 runs each).
- **The tutorial duel (v0.4.3): learning the new verse by heart.** The first story fight is Bharadvāja's duel at the Tamasā, framed as what Vālmīki tells (Bāla-kāṇḍa 2.19: the disciple, delighted, takes up the guru's new words as he speaks them, and the guru is pleased): before walking back, Bharadvāja and the student learn the brand-new verse by heart, line by line. It is call and response — Bharadvāja says a pāda in full (printed, no gap, no input), the student says it back with one word to tap (a level-0 pāda gets its last word as the gap) — one turn per pāda for a good reciter; he never strikes, so it cannot be lost. Every beat of it waits for a tap (▶); the first recitation has a one-line coach (पादं शृणु। रिक्ते स्थाने उचितं पदं चिनु।) and its gap and chips pulse until the tap. Afterwards: "Now the verse lives in two memories. Let us tell the guru." → Vālmīki, pleased, sends the student to the shrine. The practice game keeps its debate and its wording.
- **A strategic layer (v0.5a, notes/katha.md → "v0.5a").** The owner's Bāla-kāṇḍa playtest: "just spamming Rāma with the āgneyāstra was effective … there wasn't really a strategic element there." Four light rules, Kathā wild / boss fights only (duels and the practice game unchanged; `content/katha.json → tactics`, logic-tactics.ts): **(1) the right astra for the right foe** — where Vālmīki names it, the fitting astra is decisive with a short story beat (Bāla 1.30.17–22: the Mānava hurls Mārīca a hundred yojanas into the ocean, alive; the Āgneya fells Subāhu; Yuddha 6.108: the brahmāstra, once Rāvaṇa is worn to half); the foe card names each foe's affinity (उचितम्: मानव, else the element strong against it); Viśvāmitra's Mānava and Vāyavya reach Rāma when Tāṭakā falls, and Rāma leads the sacrifice fight. **(2) read the warning, answer it** — a boss readies a great blow one turn ahead in one short Sanskrit line (future tense: "… प्रयोक्ष्यते!", Tāṭakā's rain of stones, Subāhu's rain of blood); it lands on its next action, after the hero; a support move, a counter (Rāma's arrows against the stones, Bāla 1.26.17), a switch, Lakṣmaṇa's cover from the bench (one tap) or the **verse shield** (recite a line of a heard verse, least practised first) blunts it to × 0.3 and leaves the foe open (defence −1); ignored it lands at × 2.4 of an ordinary blow, capped at 45 % of the hero's HP. **(3) astra fatigue** — the same astra on consecutive turns lands × 0.6, then × 0.4 (a श्रान्तम् cue on its button); any other move resets it; plain moves do not tire. **(4) roles** — guardians cover from the bench. ### Challenge mix per story-text language (v0.4.3)
The classic challenges (practice battle moves, story quizzes, practice recitals, the Kathā study hall) fit how the player reads. The view mirrors its story-text setting into the save as the **reader level** (`save.reader`: `sa` = संस्कृतम्, `sa-en` = संस्कृतम् + English, absent = English) with the action `set-reader`, on every start / Continue and whenever Settings change it; the setting itself stays in `prefs-v1`.
- **English:** unchanged — the chapter ramp (artha, lipi from book 1; pūraṇa 2; krama 3; viccheda 4; vibhakti 5; L4 one book earlier) and today's fallbacks.
- **संस्कृतम् + English:** never **lipi** (the player reads Devanagari). A lipi move asks a meaning (artha) instead; meanings of basic words stay; every other draw is exactly as in English.
- **संस्कृतम्:** never lipi, and **never the meaning of a basic word** (nor of a name). Every other kind is open from book 1 (a reader needs no ramp) and the harder kinds come first: a lipi move asks vibhakti / viccheda / krama / pūraṇa (seeded order); a study-hall round of five has one of each kind; artha asks only the harder verse words (धर्मज्ञः, कृतज्ञः, अनिर्वेदः, तपःस्वाध्यायनिरतम् …) with harder wrong meanings; a pūraṇa gap is a harder word when its line has one, and vibhakti never asks the role of an indeclinable when the verse has a declined or verb form. ## Sanskrit rules
- **Sambhāṣaṇa story text (optional setting).** Story lines and battle narration in simple spoken Sanskrit are model-written but **verified word by word**. Every word must be a form from the platform packs (`@content/shabda`, `vocab` with paradigm links, `dhatu` finite forms/kṛdantas/lyap/prefixed verbs, `sentences`), the verse lexicon, names declined on reviewed models, or `cardinal` numbers. Other rules: padaccheda (no sandhi between words); Devanagari only; `{g:masc|fem}` for words addressing the student, both verified; full coverage of story strings. Grammar beyond word forms was reviewed by two independent model reviewers and is listed for a human teacher in `REVIEW.md`.
- **Sanskrit interface (v0.3.0).** In संस्कृतम् mode every interface string (buttons, labels, hints, toasts, battle menus, challenge prompts and explanations, card labels, grammar labels, chapter cards, shop, shrine, menu, settings, ending) is Sanskrit, from `content/sa-ui-*.json`, `content/sa-dialogue-4.json` and `content/sa-grammar.json`, verified word by word with the same oracle. Numbers use Devanagari digits. English remains only for the meaning being taught (glosses, meaning options, the verse translation), citations, the language picker's "English" and a typed Latin name, each marked `data-en-ok`. Rules: `notes/sa-ui.md`.
- **Closed world.** All Sanskrit shown or accepted comes from this game's content files (`content/*.json`, listed below with sources) or the `@sanskrit` engine. Code contains no Sanskrit literals except the manifest title. Content is Devanagari (NFC) only; IAST is derived with `devaToIast` / `<SaText>` and follows the user's Script setting everywhere.
- **Verses are letter-for-letter** from the cited corpus. They are never edited to scan. Anomalies are recorded in `meterNote`.
- **Engine confirmation.** Every pāda is scanned with `checkAnustubh`. Pādas are rebuilt from their pada-ccheda words with `joinSandhiDeva`, and exceptions are listed in `REVIEW.md` with reasons. Every string passes `validateDeva`.
- **Play units.** Words (pada-ccheda forms) and pādas. Krama challenges accept every order that yields the verse's word sequence: duplicate words are interchangeable. The challenge asks for the verse's own order, which is unique, rather than any grammatical order.
- **No generated Sanskrit.** Distractors are other real items from the content: other meanings, other forms, other real sandhi splits, other role labels. Never corrupted spellings or invented splits. Tests prove that exactly the keyed answers are correct and that no distractor equals an accepted answer. Sanskrit options are compared with the platform's `spellingKey` / `sameSpelling`, so the anusvāra and class-nasal spellings of one word (किंचित् / किञ्चित्) are one word: never two options, and the accepted set is closed under `sameSpelling` (QA 11 Nov).
- **Edition variants.** A verse word whose reading differs between witnesses (REVIEW.md human-review list) carries an English `variant` note in the content and is never the gap of a pūraṇa challenge, so the other reading is never offered or judged as wrong, until a human reviewer decides (QA 11 Nov).
- **Participles and adverbs in vibhakti.** A predicative participle keyed role "verb" whose grammar names a case (e.g. past passive participle, nom. sg. n.) is accepted under that case too, its syncretic cases are never distractors, and the feedback explains it as a participle (a verbal adjective with case, number and gender, no person ending), not as a finite verb. An indeclinable ending in -m (adverbial accusative such as nityam) never has "Accusative" as a wrong option. Demonstrative pronouns are never offered as wrong meanings of each other when one gloss includes "he, she, it" (QA 11 Nov).
- **Homographs and vikalpa.** If two content items share a form or a meaning, both are accepted or the pairing is avoided.
- **Names** (people, places, astras, items, elements, challenge kinds) are attested words or proper nouns, each with a Monier-Williams or Rāmāyaṇa citation in `content/names.json`. No coined compounds.
- **Human review.** Nothing is marked human-reviewed. `REVIEW.md` lists every item awaiting a Sanskrit scholar.

## Modes, levels, scoring
- Mode: `practice` only (campaign). Levels tune difficulty, not story:
  - **L2**: 3 options, ×1.5 timers, IAST hints under Devanagari stimuli.
  - **L3**: standard (4 options; 15 s / 25 s timers).
  - **L4**: 4 options, ×0.8 timers; viccheda and vibhakti unlock one chapter earlier (in संस्कृतम् every kind but lipi is open from book 1: see Challenge mix per story-text language).
- Score per session: 10 × correct answers + 25 × verses restored + 100 × chapters completed. Stars come from accuracy (shell default).
- Mastery ids: `shabdakosha:ramayana:<lexId>` for every answered word; `subhashita:ramayana:<shlokaId>` for every restored verse.
- No daily mode, so no share card.

## UX notes
- **"Two-screen" retro-modern layout** (DS-style), portrait-phone first.
  - Top: the 320×180 pixel canvas (overworld/battle), scaled crisp to the width.
  - Bottom: the context panel built from `@ui` components (dialogue with portrait, choices, verse/word cards, challenge options via `Choice`, krama tiles, `FeedbackBar`, battle commands, D-pad + A/B).
- **Story text setting** (Menu → Settings and the New-journey screen): English · संस्कृतम् + English (Sanskrit with the English below) · संस्कृतम् (Sanskrit only). Since v0.3.0 the setting covers the whole game, not only the story: in संस्कृतम् the interface, toasts, battle labels (a DOM overlay over the canvas) and grammar labels are Sanskrit too, and the default name is addressed as शिष्य / शिष्ये. Sanskrit lines follow the platform script (Devanagari by default, IAST if chosen). The typewriter advances by akṣara, and a missing translation falls back to English.
- **Show the way (v0.4.2)**: a gold marker on the map points to where to go next — a ▼ over the person to talk to, a glowing bracketed tile (a place, a shrine, or the exit toward the next map), or an arrow at the screen edge when it is off-screen. It follows the game's own walkthrough (the first step not done yet; across maps, the exit on the shortest route, avoiding encounter grass in the practice game when possible), in both journeys. Hidden in dialogue, verse moments, battle and menus; static with reduced motion. Settings → *Show the way* / मार्गं दिश (on by default; a preference in `prefs-v1`, never in the save).
- **Session result strings (v0.5a)**: a reader of संस्कृतम् (`save.reader` = sa) gets the game-provided strings of the shell's result screen in Sanskrit — the summary / share line (बालकाण्डं समाप्तम् · श्रुताः श्लोकाः ५ · पठिताः पादाः १६ · पूर्णाः श्लोकाः २; practice: … · पूर्णाः श्लोकाः n · उचितानि उत्तराणि c/t) and the Kathā review rows (first pāda, citation बालकाण्डम् १.२.१५, श्रुतः · पठिताः पादाः n · पूर्णः), in the player's script. Same contract (outcome, score, correct / total, best, mastery). The shell's own headings and buttons (What you practised, Share, Play again, Back to games) are platform English.
- Works at 360 px wide, keyboard-only (arrows/WASD, Z/Enter = A, X/Esc = B, M = menu, 1–4 = options), in both scripts, light and dark, and with reduced motion (no tweens, typewriter or shake).
- Sound: shell `ctx.sfx` for effects. Original chiptune music (rāga-flavoured scales) only when the platform sound setting is on, with an in-game toggle.
- Respect: heroes and sages are depicted with dignity. Sītā never fights. Violence and the abduction are narrated, not shown. Vālī's death is not gamified. A hero at 0 HP is *śrānta* (exhausted).
