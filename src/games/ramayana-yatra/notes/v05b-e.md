## The text (accuracy first)

  verse s-aditya-hrdayam (6.105.4), which is verse 4 here verbatim (test). IITK / some southern editions: sarga 107.
- **Baroda critical edition: NOT VERIFIED.** It is generally reported that the CE leaves the hymn out of the
  constituted text and prints it in the Yuddhakāṇḍa appendix (App. I). I could not reach the edition or a source that

  [paywalled], wisdomlib, a Wikipedia mirror on IPFS [no mention]; the sanskritdocuments.org CE mirror's sarga 105 is
  the gods' praise after the fire ordeal, consistent with a different sarga count but not proof). The JSON says
  "NOT VERIFIED"; a reviewer with the CE should confirm or correct it.
- Witnesses: vr = valmikiramayan.net (base), iitk = IIT Kanpur text via vedapath.app (6.107; e-text with many typos),
  sd = sanskritdocuments.org stotra (cites Gita Press 1995; spot checks), gm = greenmesg.org stotra (full).
- Rule (also in the JSON `choice`): where vr and iitk agree their reading stands; where they differ the majority of all
  witnesses decides; plain typos of one witness are corrected from the others; spelling-only differences follow vr.
- Readings chosen against vr: उपागम्य (2), भास्करो for अहस्करो (12), हर्यश्वाय (17), मार्ताण्डाय for प्रचण्डाय (18), वेदाश्च
  for देवाश्च (24). vr typos corrected: तिमिरोन्मथनः (11), ऋग्यजुःसामपारगः (13), नमोऽस्तु (15), हिमघ्नाय (20).
  Rāmāyaṇa readings kept against the stotra prints: विजयिष्यसे (3), मङ्गल्यं (5), महेन्द्रो (8), प्रजाः प्राण (9),
  हिरण्यरेता (10), मार्तण्डकोऽंशुमान् (11), नमोऽस्तु ते (18), सूर्याय (19), हरये (21), सर्वेषु परमप्रभुः (24), स (27),
  युद्धार्थं (30). Every difference of every witness is listed per verse (`variants`: 27 readings, 21 spellings, 15 typos);

- Metre (engine): 120 anuṣṭubh pādas + verse 31 in puṣpitāgrā (Vṛttaratnākara 4.10: odd na-na-ra-ya, even na-ja-ja-ra-ga;

  16.3 (all with a heavy 4th syllable, classical vipulās); 10.4 हिरण्यरेता दिवाकरः has 9 syllables in every source

## The scene
- Trigger (logic-cutscene `cutsceneDue`): Rāvaṇa's battle in book 6, both journeys, the save without `seen.aditya`, the
  first calm moment Rāvaṇa is SHOWN at ≤ 60 % — the command menu, or the narration beat on screen (its HP snapshot), so
  a hit crossing 60 % and 50 % at once still plays the scene before WP-C's heads line. Text: 6.105.1–2 (Agastya comes
  mid-fight as the weary Rāma sees Rāvaṇa back, ready to fight — 6.104 his charioteer had driven him off), before the
  heads grow back (6.107) and the brahmāstra (6.108). The view plays it, then dispatches `cutscene-done` → the flag.
  Never twice (the flag); a refight after it was seen does not replay it (the verse book does).
- Practice guard: the practice logic bot never dispatches the action (logic-sim checks style.katha), so the practice
  hashes are unchanged; in the practice game the VIEW still plays the scene (both journeys) and sets the flag, which
  nothing in practice reads.
- 3D: raw WebGL 1 (cutscene3d.ts), no dependency. Why not CSS 3D: a perspective camera that orbits and rolls, a sky and
  sea with per-pixel sun light and glitter, and sprites depth-sorted on the ground are natural in shaders; ~15 draw
  calls at ≤ 420 px wide internal resolution, scaled up with crisp pixels (cheap; the game's look). The battle canvas
  is a plane in the world: the camera starts exactly in front of it (the first 3D frame is the battle frame), pulls
  back, lifts and rolls while the plane folds back onto the ground and dissolves; the return ends on the plane again.
  Scene: dawn sky (dithered bands), the rising sun at the centre (it climbs during the hymn), the sea with the sun's
  glitter path, the shore and battlefield, Laṅkā on Trikūṭa, Rāvaṇa's chariot far off, Rāma and Agastya as 4-way
  pixel billboards (the game's own sprites; the frame follows the camera angle), clouds and the watching gods as points
  of light. Shots follow the text (chariot for Rāvaṇa's return, close on Rāma, the sky for the gods, Agastya walks in,
  an orbit for the epithets, the sun for the salutations, Rāvaṇa again as Rāma takes his bow, a crane up to the sun).
- Fallbacks: reduced motion / no WebGL / lost context → a still painting of the same scene with crossfades
  (paintScene2D); context restored → back to 3D. Music: the existing 'shloka' track (tanpura drone; follows mute).
- Follow-along: the narration, then every pāda lit with a sweep at 3 akṣaras / s (RECITE_RATE; pādas ≥ 1.5 s; pauses
  after pāda / half-verse / verse), auto-scrolled; the verse counter (श्लोकः ४ / ३१), Agastya's tag on 3–27, the
  current verse's English; ≈ 7 min in all. Pause / resume (button, Space, Z, P), skip (button, Esc / X) → "Skip the
  hymn?" Yes / No (focus on No); a skip still flies back and counts as seen.
- English in संस्कृतम्: the narration's and the verses' English carry data-en-ok (the owner's request: "showing
  Sanskrit text and English translation … the same across the language versions"); the controls follow the language.
  The e2e no-Latin sweep (latinOnScreen) already skips data-en-ok, so no list in e2e-kit had to change.
- Replay (the easter egg): Menu → श्लोकाः shows "आदित्यहृदयम् ▶" once seen; it closes the menu, plays the scene from
  the current map frame, and reopens the verse book. No hidden second way: the game has no map for Agastya's āśrama
  (the ch6 Agastya stands on the battlefield), so "facing the sun at Agastya's āśrama" has no place to live.
- e2e: ?e2e=1 runs it fast (window.__ryCutsceneSpeed sets the pace live) and logs `phase|step` to window.__ryCutsceneLog;
  expose.cutscene = { id, replay } while on.
